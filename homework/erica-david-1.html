Intro

The most important thing, is finding a way to figure out what the 'right' play is. Some people are naturally gifted, like Faker, at just playing and diagnosing while playing. Which means for players like him, more playtime = better player.

Sadly, we are not Faker.

It's important to recognize ones own skillset in life and work around it. Regardless of whether we like the hand we are dealt, it is up to us to make the most of it. Both of you, you both have significantly higher play time than Adriene/Matthew, however I would say their level of play is higher.

Given that, I would say both of you fall into the same category of player as me. You have strong micro, but have difficulties figuring out what the right/wrong play through just playtime/self vod review. The best way to improve for people like us is to have a teacher/examples to follow.

Mindset changes

Historically, you both play extremely risk averse. To me it always seemed like you want to avoid making mistakes, however you do so in a way that puts you at a disadvantage in the game.

The first ask I have, is for you to accept:

It is better to make mistakes and go 0/10, than avoid making mistakes and go 0/1

You will lose RP in short term, but long term it is the only way to improve as a player. Playing to avoid mistakes hinders your own ability to figure out what the 'right' play is, because you never present it as an option to yourself.

Actual Homework

To speed up your learning, let's break down the game into 3 sections:

  1. Laning phase/early game (before either bot tower is broken)
  2. Mid game (think teamfighting over objectives + post laning phase)
  3. Late game (think when everyone is full/near full build and you kinda just sit around waiting for baron to spawn) ← this is pretty rare, and should be the least of your focus when studying

Your first focus should be learning laning phase/early game. ONLY once you have a good handle of #1, we can move onto learning #2. Don't try to learn everything at once, they didn't teach you algebra in preschool for a reason.

This homework is focused on laning phase, so I want you to take the following approach for your next 2 weeks of flex duo queue:

  1. Find a high elo vod or edited gameplay (e.g. bausffs) for your champ (preferrably recent patches), watch it and try to understand their laning phase play style
  2. Play the champ, and try to copy the laning phase from the vod
  3. After you win/lose try and find a high elo vod for the specific matchup and watch the laning phase and see the high elo player did differently.

Concepts

  1. What does 'play style' mean when it comes to watching vods/copying playstyle? It depends per role, but for both ADC/Supp:

    1. What's their plan with laning phase → e.g. lets say you're draven blitz against seraphine sona. You know you have high all in potential, however you lose long term trades. Given that theres a bunch of options you can do. E.g. do you take fleet footwork + longsword 3 pots on draven trying to sustain and farm? Do you take hail of blades and look to freeze wave near your tower and zone off CS? The only way to know is to come up with plans and experiment. If you autopilot your laning phase.
    2. How/When do they trade (you can break this down by thinking):
      1. Who wins short trades
      2. Who wins long trades
      3. Who wins all ins
      4. Do any of the above change depending on level? E.g. lucian nami level 2 is one of the strongest in the game
    3. Who gets push/where do you want the lane to end up
    4. How should you space (wrt minions, enemy laners, your other laner)
